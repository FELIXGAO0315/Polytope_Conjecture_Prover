**Statement.** Let $M$ be a simply connected $3$-connected map, and for each $k \geq 3$ let $p_k$ denote the number of faces of $M$ of size $k$. Writing $f_2$ for the total number of faces, we claim that
$$p_6 \;\leq\; 2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(2f_2 - 4) - p_4 - p_5.$$
Equivalently, after clearing the factor of $2$, we prove
$$2\,p_6 \;\leq\; 2f_2 - 2p_4 - 2p_5,$$
where $f_2 = \sum_{k=3}^{m} p_k$ is the total face count and $m$ is the maximum face size occurring in $M$.

**Proof.** The argument rests on a single elementary decomposition of the face sum, followed by a non-negativity bound.

*Step 1: Splitting the face sum.* We first isolate the low-order terms in the total face count. Since every face has size at least $3$, we may write
$$\sum_{k=3}^{m} p_k \;=\; p_3 + p_4 + p_5 + p_6 + \sum_{k=7}^{m} p_k.$$
To justify this identity rigorously, we extend the range of summation up to $N := \max(7, m+1)$; because $p_k = 0$ for every $k > m$, padding the sums with these vanishing terms changes nothing. On the extended range we may split $\{3,4,\dots,N-1\}$ at the index $7$, obtaining
$$\sum_{k=3}^{N-1} p_k \;=\; \sum_{k=3}^{6} p_k \;+\; \sum_{k=7}^{N-1} p_k,$$
and the initial block expands as $p_3 + p_4 + p_5 + p_6$. Removing the padding on each side yields the stated decomposition. This is the lemma **C124_SumSplit**.

*Step 2: Bounding $p_6$ by the residual sum.* From the decomposition above, subtract $p_4 + p_5$ from both sides:
$$\Bigl(\sum_{k=3}^{m} p_k\Bigr) - p_4 - p_5 \;=\; p_3 + p_6 + \sum_{k=7}^{m} p_k.$$
Every term on the right is a face count, hence non-negative. Dropping $p_3$ and $\sum_{k \geq 7} p_k$ therefore only weakens the right-hand side, giving
$$p_6 \;\leq\; \Bigl(\sum_{k=3}^{m} p_k\Bigr) - p_4 - p_5.$$

*Step 3: Conclusion.* Multiplying through by $2$ yields
$$2\,p_6 \;\leq\; 2\sum_{k=3}^{m} p_k \;-\; 2p_4 \;-\; 2p_5,$$
which is the desired inequality. Rewriting the total face count as $f_2$ and rearranging recovers the form stated at the outset:
$$p_6 \;\leq\; 2 + \tfrac{1}{2}(2f_2 - 4) - p_4 - p_5.$$

∎
