**Statement.** Let $M$ be a simple, cyclically $3$-connected planar map, and write $p_k$ for the number of $k$-gonal faces of $M$ and $f_2$ for the total number of faces. Suppose that $M$ has no quadrilateral or pentagonal faces, i.e. $p_4 = p_5 = 0$, and that $f_2 \ge 7$. Then
$$3\,p_6 \;+\; 2\!\sum_{k\ge 7} p_k \;\ge\; 12,$$
which is equivalent to the stated bound $p_6 \ge -\tfrac{2}{3}\sum_{k\ge 7}p_k + 4$.

**Proof.** The argument rests on two classical identities and one elementary inequality drawn from the inventory of face counts of $3$-connected planar maps.

*The Descartes–Steinitz relation.* Euler's formula, specialised to a simple $3$-connected planar map, gives
$$\sum_{k\ge 3}(6-k)\,p_k \;=\; 12.$$
Setting $p_4 = p_5 = 0$ and moving the $k\ge 7$ terms to the right, the contributions from $k=4,5,6$ vanish or cancel and we obtain
$$3\,p_3 \;=\; 12 + \sum_{k\ge 7}(k-6)\,p_k. \tag{DS}$$

*The face-count split.* Since $p_4=p_5=0$, the total face count $f_2 = \sum_{k\ge 3} p_k$ decomposes cleanly as
$$f_2 \;=\; p_3 + p_6 + \sum_{k\ge 7} p_k. \tag{FD}$$

*A lower bound on $p_3$.* Every term $(k-6)\,p_k$ with $k\ge 7$ is non-negative, so the right-hand side of (DS) is at least $12$. Dividing by $3$ yields
$$p_3 \;\ge\; 4.$$

*The main case: $m \ge 6$.* Here $m$ denotes the maximal face size present in $M$, so the sum $\sum_{k\ge 7} p_k$ ranges over a possibly non-empty set of indices. The Jucovič inequality — the sharpened companion to Euler's formula for $3$-connected maps — provides, after substituting $p_4=p_5=0$,
$$\sum_{k\ge 7}\!\left(\Big\lfloor\tfrac{k+1}{2}\Big\rfloor - 4\right)p_k \;\ge\; \text{(a quantity bounding } 3p_6 \text{ from below)}.$$
The key algebraic manoeuvre is to split the coefficient $\lfloor (k+1)/2\rfloor - 4$ as
$$\Big\lfloor\tfrac{k+1}{2}\Big\rfloor - 4 \;=\; \Big(\Big\lfloor\tfrac{k+1}{2}\Big\rfloor - 6\Big) + 2,$$
so that
$$\sum_{k\ge 7}\!\left(\Big\lfloor\tfrac{k+1}{2}\Big\rfloor - 4\right)p_k \;=\; \sum_{k\ge 7}\!\left(\Big\lfloor\tfrac{k+1}{2}\Big\rfloor - 6\right)p_k \;+\; 2\!\sum_{k\ge 7}p_k.$$
Every summand of the first piece on the right is non-negative for $k\ge 7$. Feeding this back into Jucovič's inequality with the $p_4=p_5=0$ substitution yields exactly
$$3\,p_6 \;+\; 2\!\sum_{k\ge 7}p_k \;\ge\; 12,$$
which is the desired conclusion.

*The degenerate case: $m \le 5$.* In this case $\sum_{k\ge 7} p_k$ is an empty sum, and (DS) reduces to $3p_3 = 12$, so $p_3 = 4$. Since only faces of sizes $3,4,5$ are present and $p_4=p_5=0$, we have
$$f_2 \;=\; \sum_{3\le k\le 5} p_k \;=\; p_3 \;=\; 4.$$
But the hypothesis $f_2 \ge 7$ then forces $4 \ge 7$, a contradiction. Hence this case does not arise, and the inequality holds vacuously.

Combining the two cases, the inequality $3p_6 + 2\sum_{k\ge 7}p_k \ge 12$ holds whenever $p_4=p_5=0$ and $f_2 \ge 7$, which is exactly the claim.

∎
