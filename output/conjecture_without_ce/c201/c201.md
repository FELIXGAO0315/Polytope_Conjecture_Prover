**Statement.** Let $M$ be a simply connected $3$-connected planar map, and write $p_k$ for the number of $k$-gonal faces of $M$. Assume that $M$ has no quadrilateral or pentagonal faces, i.e. $p_4 = 0$ and $p_5 = 0$, and that $M$ has at least one face of size $\geq 7$, so that $S := \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k \geq 1$. Then
$$p_6 \;\geq\; -S + 2 \;=\; -\sum_{k \geq 7} p_k \;+\; 2.$$

**Proof.** The argument proceeds by a case split on the size of the tail sum $S = \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k$.

*The case $S \geq 2$.* If at least two faces of size $\geq 7$ are present, then the claimed lower bound $-S + 2$ is already $\leq 0$. Since $p_6$ is a count of faces and hence non-negative, the inequality $p_6 \geq -S + 2$ holds trivially.

*The case $S = 1$.* Suppose exactly one face has size $\geq 7$. It suffices to prove $p_6 \geq 1$, since then $p_6 \geq 1 = -S + 2$.

First we observe that the map cannot be too small: if the maximum face size $m$ were at most $5$, the index set $\{k : 7 \leq k \leq m\}$ would be empty and $S$ would vanish, contradicting $S = 1$. Hence $m \geq 6$, which puts us in the regime where the general $p_6$-inequality of the framework applies. That inequality gives, after clearing the contributions of $p_4$ and $p_5$ (both zero by hypothesis), a bound of the form
$$p_6 \;\geq\; C \;+\; \sum_{k \geq 7} \left(\left\lfloor \tfrac{k+1}{2} \right\rfloor - 6 \right) p_k,$$
with a constant $C$ contributed by the structural part.

To exploit this we bound the tail weighted sum from below. For every $k \geq 7$ one has $\lfloor (k+1)/2 \rfloor - 6 \geq -2$, and $p_k \geq 0$, so
$$\sum_{k \geq 7} \left(\left\lfloor \tfrac{k+1}{2} \right\rfloor - 6 \right) p_k \;\geq\; -2 \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k \;=\; -2S \;=\; -2.$$
Substituting this into the structural inequality and using $S = 1$ yields $p_6 \geq 1$, as required.

*Combining the cases.* Both branches of the case split produce $p_6 \geq -S + 2$, which is the desired inequality.

Two additional ingredients underlie the $S = 1$ argument and were established as auxiliary lemmas. From the Dehn–Sommerville relation for maps of low face degree, the vanishing of $p_4$ and $p_5$ gives the identity
$$3 p_3 \;=\; 12 \;+\; \sum_{k \geq 7} (k - 6)\, p_k,$$
and since each coefficient $k - 6 \geq 1$ for $k \geq 7$, one has the domination $\sum_{k \geq 7} (k-6) p_k \geq \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k = S$. Together these give $3 p_3 \geq 12 + S$, so in particular $p_3 \geq 5$ whenever $S \geq 1$. Similarly, one has the face-count decomposition
$$\sum_{k \geq 3} p_k \;=\; p_3 + p_6 + \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k,$$
obtained by removing the vanishing $p_4$ and $p_5$ terms. These pieces support the structural $p_6$-inequality used above and close the $S = 1$ case.

∎
