**Statement.** Let $M$ be a simply connected $3$-connected planar map, and write $p_k$ for the number of $k$-gonal faces of $M$ and $m$ for its maximum face size. Assume that $M$ has no quadrilateral faces ($p_4 = 0$), at most two pentagonal faces ($p_5 \leq 2$), and at least seven faces in total ($\sum_{k \geq 3} p_k \geq 7$). Then, writing $S := \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k$ for the number of faces of size at least $7$,
$$2\,p_6 \;\geq\; 4 - S \;=\; 4 - \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k.$$

**Proof.** If $S \geq 4$ the right-hand side is $\leq 0$, and the inequality holds trivially since $p_6 \geq 0$. Assume therefore $S \leq 3$.

*The map is not degenerate: $m \geq 6$.* Suppose to the contrary that every face had size at most $5$. Then all faces are triangles, quadrilaterals or pentagons, so the hypothesis $\sum_k p_k \geq 7$ together with $p_4 = 0$ and $p_5 \leq 2$ forces $p_3 \geq 5$. On the other hand, the Euler-type identity of Jučovič,
$$3\,p_3 \;=\; 12 - 2\,p_4 - p_5 + \sum_{k \geq 7} (k-6)\,p_k,$$
has an empty tail sum when $m < 6$, and with $p_4 = 0$ it reduces to $3\,p_3 = 12 - p_5 \leq 12$ — contradicting $3\,p_3 \geq 15$. Hence $m \geq 6$, and the structural inequalities of the framework apply.

*The main bound.* By Jučovič's inequality (valid for $m \geq 6$), after removing the vanishing $p_4$ term,
$$3\,p_6 \;\geq\; 12 - 3\,p_5 + \sum_{k \geq 7} \left(\left\lfloor \tfrac{k+1}{2} \right\rfloor - 6\right) p_k.$$
For every $k \geq 7$ the coefficient satisfies $\lfloor (k+1)/2 \rfloor - 6 \geq -2$, and $p_k \geq 0$, so the tail sum is at least $-2S$. Combining with $p_5 \leq 2$,
$$3\,p_6 \;\geq\; 12 - 3\,p_5 - 2S \;\geq\; 6 - 2S.$$

*The case $S \leq 2$.* Here the main bound and integrality of $p_6$ already suffice: for $S = 0$ it gives $p_6 \geq 2$; for $S = 1$ it gives $3\,p_6 \geq 4$, hence $p_6 \geq 2$; for $S = 2$ it gives $3\,p_6 \geq 2$, hence $p_6 \geq 1$. In each case $2\,p_6 \geq 4 - S$, as required.

*The case $S = 3$.* Now the target is $2\,p_6 \geq 1$, and Jučovič's bound alone only yields $3\,p_6 \geq 0$; the second $p_6$-bound of Barnette, applicable because $\sum_{k\geq 7} p_k \geq 3$, closes the gap. It states
$$2\,p_6 \;\geq\; 4 + p_3 - p_5 - 2 \sum_{k \geq 7} p_k \;=\; p_3 - p_5 - 2,$$
so it remains to bound $p_3$ from below. In the Jučovič identity above, each tail coefficient satisfies $k - 6 \geq 1$ for $k \geq 7$, so the tail sum dominates $\sum_{k \geq 7} p_k = 3$; with $p_4 = 0$ this gives
$$3\,p_3 \;\geq\; 12 - p_5 + 3 \;=\; 15 - p_5 \;\geq\; 13,$$
hence $p_3 \geq 5$ by integrality. Substituting into Barnette's bound, $2\,p_6 \geq 5 - 2 - 2 = 1 = 4 - S$, which is the claim.

*Combining the cases.* All branches produce $2\,p_6 \geq 4 - S$, completing the proof.

∎
